## Analysis & Presentation

- [x] **Establishing baselines** You DO actually need to check how accurate you would get just fitting the data with least squares or some other simple parametric method. You can do this via an MSE on the data given the model (likelihood of the data). **Done** -- `smi/analysis/baseline_fit.py`, report at `report/baseline.typ`. Likelihood and MSE turn out to be the same computation under Gaussian residuals, so the closed-form fit is both the simple and the rigorous option. Baseline (method 2, N=200): displacement RMSE 0.433 um free space / 0.462 um fiber, velocity RMSE 0.488 / 0.550 **um/ms** (these were recorded as 488 / 550 um/s before the units convention was settled). The artifact has been regenerated under the new key names and `schema_version` 3, and method 2's medians came back identical, which is the check that splitting the two methods' shot counts left the method-2 path alone. Method 1 now runs at N=1000 and method 2 stays at N=200, because method 2 is ~5.85 s/shot; both counts are in the artifact's `config` block and no table should be read under the other's N. This is directly comparable to a training log with no conversion, which was the point of the units work above. ~~The forward-model residual says the Michelson model explains only 9-38% of the variance with displacement known~~ -- **retracted**: that was a global-fit artifact, see the follow-up below.
    - [ ] **Why does the Michelson model fit this data so badly?** **Largely answered: it was the estimator, not the model.** `forward_model_residual` fit one amplitude/phase/offset across all 16384 samples (33.6 ms), which no single phase can do when a shot drifts 1-2.7 fringes within the record. Refitting per window of W=1024 samples and pooling the variances lifts the large-excursion cohort (N=1000, top 200 by excursion, live `k`) from R^2 0.013-0.063 to 0.272-0.781. `windowed_forward_model_residual` and its mandatory `scrambled_theta_control` are committed, and the artifact now reports global, windowed and control side by side per channel. In the committed artifact (all 1000 shots, live `k`) the medians go global -> windowed (control): free space 0.171 -> 0.715 (0.298) / 0.176 -> 0.629 (0.330) / 0.080 -> 0.422 (0.238); mm fiber 0.479 -> 0.863 (0.332) / 0.166 -> 0.602 (0.315) / 0.303 -> 0.730 (0.252). **The specific retracted claim was that every channel collapses to R^2 <= 0.11 above 2 um.** The >3 um bin now holds 134 and 143 shots (against dozens at N=200) and reads 0.265-0.777 windowed against controls of 0.186-0.280.
        - **W=1024 is chosen by the control, not by taste.** The scrambled-theta control (same window count, same 3 parameters, theta from a different shot) rises with shorter windows as pure fitting capacity: 0.006-0.009 at W=16384, 0.185-0.274 at W=1024, 0.569-0.681 at W=256. W=1024 maximizes the real-vs-control gap (up to 0.543); W=256's numbers must never be quoted as model agreement. Measured controls at W=1024 run about 2x higher than the spec that commissioned this work predicted (0.088-0.117) -- the windowed-vs-control *gap* and the choice of W both hold, but the absolute control figure in that spec does not reproduce, and no variant of the pairing (neighbour, small-excursion, random, time-reversed) gets near it. A 1024-sample window sweeps a median 2.17 fringes, so a 3-parameter fit against any oscillation of comparable rate captures ~0.2 by arithmetic; a white-noise control matches the theoretical `3/W` exactly (0.0028 vs 0.0029), which is what rules out an implementation error.
        - **Report per channel, never averaged.** Free-space 515 nm is the one case where W=1024 does not establish agreement: real 0.272 against a control of 0.185, a gap of just 0.087. Averaging the three channels would report 0.400 for that acquisition and hide it completely.
        - **The gain error is real, secondary, and deliberately not fixed here.** `smi/analysis/gain_sweep.py` sweeps a multiplier on the coil-driver constant `k` (101 values, 10 to 35, N=1000) and writes `smi/analysis/data/gain_sweep_results.json`. Median R^2 peaks sharply above the live 14.553: best `k` = 16.50 / 16.25 / 16.00 at 635 / 675 / 515 nm free space and 15.50 / 15.75 / 15.25 in the fiber, i.e. the displacement targets are 4.8-13.4% too small. Per-bin global R^2 at live -> best `k`, 635 nm: free space 1-2 um 0.192 -> 0.560, 2-3 um 0.037 -> 0.323, >3 um 0.028 -> 0.122; mm fiber 1-2 um 0.535 -> 0.707, 2-3 um 0.242 -> 0.541, >3 um 0.045 -> 0.225. Once the estimator is right the correction is small: windowed R^2 moves only 0.712 -> 0.767 / 0.626 -> 0.651 / 0.422 -> 0.435 (free space) and 0.860 -> 0.883 / 0.600 -> 0.617 / 0.726 -> 0.746 (fiber). The commented-out `k=33.42` from the 2024 calibration is refuted at R^2 0.012-0.029, so the file's own note "Incorrect fringe counts in 2024 calibration?" was right. **`k` in `smi/synthetic/calib_params.py` was NOT changed**: it would shift every training target, and the two acquisitions disagree about the right value (below), so it needs its own experiment and its own evidence bar.
        - **Two caveats that survive.** (1) The acquisitions disagree on best `k` by 3.2-6.5% per channel (16.50 vs 15.50 at 635 nm, 16.25 vs 15.75 at 675 nm, 16.00 vs 15.25 at 515 nm). The sweep step is 0.25, 1.7% of the live gain, so the disagreement is wider than the resolution; drive-to-displacement gain is a property of the speaker, not the optical path, so a scalar `k` is absorbing something else. (2) **A per-shot fitted gain does not rescue the collapse.** Given each shot its own optimum, the free-space >3 um bin still sits at R^2 0.143 / 0.094 / 0.052, and the best single global gain gives 0.122 / 0.076 / 0.020. That is what rules out a scale error and leaves unmodeled within-shot drift as the explanation. Read the per-shot medians with care: fringe-order aliases at roughly twice the true gain inflate them, most on mm-fiber 675 nm (249 of 1000 shots pick 29.5-32.5, 334 pick above 25) and free-space 515 nm (125 above 25).
        - **Ruled out, with committed checks rather than recollection.** Time delay: shifting the prediction over +/-8 samples gives no interior peak in any of the six channel/acquisition combinations, and the verdict is taken from peak prominence rather than from where the argmax lands (an argmax at an endpoint is what a monotonic curve returns either way). Aliasing: peak fringe rate at live `k` is 9.9-13.6 kHz against a 244 kHz Nyquist, 17.9-24.8x headroom -- this supersedes the earlier informal "~7.4 kHz", which was 635 nm only and undersampled. A missing DC term: the fit carries a constant, globally and per window. The `4*pi*d/lambda` phase factor and mis-set `f0`/`Q`: a wrong factor or resonator would move the gain peak or remove it, and instead the peak is sharp and within 13% of live in all six channels, with the best gain wavelength-independent as a mechanical error must be. Detector nonlinearity: not supported by any measurement here.
        - Still open: the residual amplitude dependence that survives a per-shot gain fit. The sub-item's headline question is answered, so the report no longer rests on it, but that residual is not explained.
